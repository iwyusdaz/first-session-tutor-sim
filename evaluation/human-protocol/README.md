# Human playtest protocol (stubs for the first real sessions)

No real tutors or learners have tested this project yet. These short templates are ready for the first sessions; nothing here has been run.

## 1. Consent script

> "You're going to try a tutoring simulation built for training. The students in it are simulated and fictional. It saves your choices only inside this browser; nothing is sent anywhere. You can stop any time. May we take notes on what you do and say, to improve the exercise? Notes are anonymized and you can ask us to delete them."

Participants are adult tutors or tutor candidates. Record only anonymized notes.

## 2. Session template (45 min)

- 0–5 min: think-aloud setup ("say what you're noticing and why you choose a move"). Pre-check items.
- 5–25 min: Run 1 (the quiet student). Record: which cues the participant mentions unprompted, whether they use the Notice checklist, how long they actually wait at m4 and m7, any move they wanted that wasn't in the palette.
- 25–35 min: Debrief replay. Ask: "Which moment would you change? Why?" Note whether the state graphs and coach notes are understood.
- 35–45 min: Run 2 (the dominant student) or the post-check. Ask afterwards which moves transferred.

## 3. Coach-facing session (optional, 30 min)

A site coordinator watches a tutor's replay and runs a coaching conversation using the observation rubric. Record whether the replay supports the conversation and what was missing.

## 4. Questions to answer

- Are the body-language cues legible and do they read as the intended states?
- Do the student behaviors and dialogue feel realistic to experienced tutors and middle-school teachers?
- Does anyone find a portrayal stereotyped?
- Is the real-time wait helpful or just frustrating?

## 5. What not to claim from early sessions

A handful of sessions can find usability and realism problems. They cannot show that the simulator improves real tutoring.
